Thirty years of faces

Many of you were with us this fall at Transform, where we celebrated 30 years of Outreach. Thirty years ago, Outreach was a minivan full of PB&J sandwiches, bottled water and hygiene supplies. When we look back now, what we remember are the people.

Samantha lost her mom and her grandmother within six months of each other. Shortly before her son's first birthday, he was removed from her care because she didn't have a safe place to live. Our team threw him a birthday party at the Program Center anyway, with cake and photos so she'd always have the day. Today she's raising her kids in a home she loves, and each of them has a bedroom of their own.

Lamar was a high school sophomore when his family lost their home. He made his Outreach coach, Rob, a promise: get me to graduation, and I will soar. Today he runs his own firm, Holliday Collaborative Agency, and sits on our Board of Directors.

And Caroncia, who spent her senior year not knowing where her family would sleep, is now a student at IU Bloomington. You'll read more about her below.

All December, we're sharing stories like theirs in a series called Where Are They Now? Outreach was there for each of them when it counted, and with your help, we'll be there for the next young person who walks through our doors.

That's why we say ending youth homelessness ends homelessness.

Outreach Thanksgiving

On Friday, November 6, young people filled Cornerstone Lutheran Church for our annual Thanksgiving dinner. Ceddrick cooked a full homemade meal. Hoosier Heritage Farm donated every turkey. The team at enVista bought the groceries, donated brand-new coats, and stayed to serve. And the congregation at Cornerstone Lutheran opened their doors and pitched in all night.

Every young person went home full, with a new coat, gloves and a hat for the winter. One of them summed it up for 13News:

They weren't the only ones. New City Church spent a Friday cooking gyros with our young people at the Program Center, and they were a hit. And Mass Ave Knit Shop hosted its first bingo night for Outreach. It sold out, and it brought in gift cards, food, coats and donations.

Thank you to the community partners and volunteers who make the holidays a little easier for our youth.

Caroncia

Caroncia spent her senior year of high school not knowing where her family would sleep. Through our ARCH program, she had a coach who helped her plan for graduation and cover her basic needs.

She graduated. This fall, she stood up at Transform and told her story, and The Mind Trust surprised her with a scholarship. Today she's a student at IU Bloomington. Watch Caroncia's story
